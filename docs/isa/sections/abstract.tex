\begin{abstract}
Metaheuristic tuning of PID-type controllers for power converters usually
searches gain spaces that carry no stability guarantee, and its results are
rarely checked against switching effects. This paper shows that, for a
non-ideal Buck converter, the linear--quadratic--integral (LQI) law has an
exact realization as a two-degree-of-freedom PIDF that measures only the output
voltage. When its derivative pole is placed on the capacitor ESR zero, the
filter state reconstructs the capacitor voltage along every trajectory. The
PIDF and the full-state LQI therefore coincide under saturation, anti-windup
and discontinuous conduction. They differ only through an explicit
output-current term, output-network mismatch and sampling, and a first-order-hold
discretization reduces the sampling gap by a factor of about 40. The controller
is tuned by searching the LQI weights, not the gains, so every candidate is
LQ-optimal and carries guaranteed margins and a certified pole region. A
constrained, multi-fidelity search includes short switched-model simulations to
reject coexisting switching limit cycles that averaged models miss. On a
\SI{48}{V} converter commanded from 1 to \SI{46}{V}, six metaheuristics converge
to the same optimum. An unconstrained gain search reaches the same controller,
but \IOpct\,\% of its feasible solutions are not LQ-optimal. On a switched
replica of the reference Simulink model, the tuned controller eliminates
overshoot and respects the device current rating, whereas standard PI, PID,
PIDF, LQR, LQG and LQI designs exceed the specification by factors of 6 to 30.
\end{abstract}
