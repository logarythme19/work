\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Digitally controlled DC--DC converters are routinely tuned by metaheuristics:
particle swarms, genetic algorithms, bee colonies and grey-wolf variants search
the gains of a PI, PID or filtered PID (PIDF) against a tracking index
\citep{Izci2022,Nanyan2024,RodriguezMolina2020}. Two weaknesses recur in that
literature, and both are visible to an industrial reader. First, the searched
object is a vector of gains with no structural meaning, so a good gain vector
carries no stability or robustness certificate and the search space is sized by
guesswork. Second, the evidence is usually produced on the same averaged model
that drives the search, so switching ripple, duty saturation and discontinuous
conduction, which decide whether a tuning is admissible on the real power stage,
never enter the comparison \citep{Middlebrook1976,Erickson2020}.

This paper addresses both points on one concrete, audited plant: a non-ideal
asynchronous Buck converter supplied from a fixed \SI{48}{V} source and
commanded over the whole attainable range \SIrange{1}{46}{V}. The design is
\emph{formula first}. The searched object is not a gain vector but the set of
Bryson excursions that define a linear--quadratic--integral (LQI) cost; every
candidate is solved from the algebraic Riccati equation and then realized
\emph{exactly} as a two-degree-of-freedom PIDF (2-DOF PIDF) that reads the output
voltage only. Every evaluated point is therefore a genuine LQI law, with the
classical LQR return-difference guarantees, and at the same time a controller
that a practitioner can implement in a standard PID block with setpoint weights.

The central theoretical observation is stronger than a transfer-function
identity. We show that the derivative filter of the realizing PIDF, when its pole
is placed at the equivalent-series-resistance (ESR) zero $N_C=1/(r_CC)$ and its
state is initialized consistently, reconstructs the capacitor voltage
\emph{exactly}, whatever happens in the inductor branch. Consequently the
output-feedback PIDF and the full-state LQI generate identical trajectories
under duty saturation, anti-windup activity, discontinuous conduction and any
mismatch of $L$, $r_L$, $R_{on}$, $r_d$ or $V_f$; only an unmeasured load current,
a mismatch of the output network $(R,r_C,C)$, or sampling break the equivalence.
This turns a nominal, small-signal equivalence into a structural statement that
can be checked on a simulator to machine precision, and it tells the
practitioner exactly which sensors the PIDF saves and at what price.

The optimization layer compares six equal-budget global operators---PSO, GA,
ABC and three project variants pAEABC, pIGWO and pIGWO--DLH---each followed by a
common Gaussian-process refinement stage (refineBO), over 30 matched seeds. The
seven algorithms are not the contribution by themselves; the question they
answer is whether the \emph{LQI-weight parameterization} helps them. We
therefore run the identical protocol on five other controller families that
share the evaluator, the budget and the specifications: the full-state twin of
the design (same weights, measured $i_L$), the same 2-DOF PIDF searched directly
in gain space, a PIDF with free filter pole and setpoint weights, a PID and a PI.
Six fixed-rule comparators---PI, PID, PIDF (Type-III rule), LQR, LQG and
LQI---complete the picture, and every frozen controller is replayed on a switched
replica of the reference Simulink/Simscape model on the complete
\SI{24}{}$\to$\SI{1}{}$\to$\SI{46}{V} mission, with a closed energy balance.

A previous version of this protocol accepted candidates on an averaged model
guarded by an analytical switching-ripple bound, and four of six branch winners
failed on the switched model. We reproduce that failure mode, show that it is
caused by coexisting switching limit cycles whose selection depends on the
preceding transient, and replace the analytical bound by three \emph{measured}
inequalities obtained from a \SI{34}{ms} switched transition sequence inside
each evaluation, a multi-fidelity constraint that roughly doubles the cost of
a candidate. Feasibility is also stated in absolute
engineering units (overshoot, load deviation, device current, steady error)
instead of ratios to a reference controller, so that every family is judged
against the same specification.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a formula-first chain from the non-ideal converter equations to the
  exact output-feedback realization of any LQI law as a 2-DOF PIDF, with all
  analytical quantities (equilibria, transfer functions, Riccati gain, LQR
  margins, a common-$P$ D-region certificate over the operating polytope)
  instantiated and independently re-computed;
\item a structural equivalence theorem (Proposition~\ref{prop:struct}) showing
  that the PIDF filter state equals the capacitor voltage along every
  trajectory of the plant, with a proof, its exact domain of validity and a
  machine-precision numerical confirmation;
\item a constrained co-design problem in the space of LQI weights with 33
  explicit inequalities in engineering units, three of which are measured on the
  switched model during the search;
\item a matched, equal-budget comparison of seven algorithms across six
  controller families, which isolates the value of the LQI parameterization
  from the value of the optimizer;
\item a switched-model validation on the reference mission against PI, PID,
  PIDF, LQR, LQG and LQI, reported with the specifications each controller
  meets or violates rather than with a single aggregate score.
\end{enumerate}
